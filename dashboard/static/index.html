Numeric columns

Each number becomes sign(x)·log1p(|x|), then is standardized with the mean and standard deviation below (of the logged finite values); a missing or infinite one lands on the mean, 0, and the n_missing column counts, unscaled, how many numbers of the row did (present even when none). It is a diagnostic: no distance sees it.

Categorical slots

The top_n most frequent values of a column (a missing one counts as a value) get their own one-hot slot; the others share the <other> slot. The active slot weighs sqrt(cat_cost / g), with g the chance two rows differ on the column: on average a column adds cat_cost times what a number adds, and a mismatch on a concentrated column costs more (2·weight² in squared distance).

Tree of a class

Each class is split on its own by a Ward tree of its distinct rows, each weighted by its copies in the CSV. Every branch is cut where the loss is least: the hardness spread a region hides (kDN variance) plus the sampling noise of its mean hardness. A branch under min_cluster_floor rows of the sample is pruned; its rows, and the rows outside the sample of the tree, take the region of their nearest labelled rows by vote. The tree shows the splits the cut keeps; each node counts the distinct rows of its regions (hover for the rows of the CSV). Click a node to map its rows.

Overlaps between classes

Judged on the regions alone. The separation of two regions is the distance between their centroids in standard deviations of their rows along the line between them (√ of Fisher's ratio on that line): two regions of different classes overlap when it is under overlap_separation. Single linkage on these pairs, the closest first, groups the regions into zones; a region close to no other class is in none, and classes that never come that close never meet. Each zone is drawn as its tree: leaves in the colour of their class, each merge at the separation of the pair that made it. Click a node to map its rows and find its branch in each class tree.

Pairs of regions that overlap

Every pair of regions of different classes under overlap_separation, not only those that made a merge; sd a and sd b are the standard deviations of each region's rows along the line between the centroids. Click a row to select the lowest node above both regions.

Granularity per class

The cut of least loss plus a penalty per region, region_cost times the loss of the class as one region: a split is kept only when it lowers the loss by at least that share. The penalty rises further when the class had to be capped at max_regions. Loss / one region is the loss of the cut over that of the class as one region. The curve is the loss of the cut against its regions as the penalty falls.

Regions

Select a dataset.

Classes

The smells of Flood et al. (EuroS&P 2024) per class, measured on its regions. Copies: CSV rows that repeat another. Conflicts: CSV rows equal on every feature to a row of another class. Overlap (ENN): rows whose nearest neighbours are mostly of the closest rival region, weighted by rows. One feature: the most of a pair's rows a single feature tells apart. Shortcut: rows of regions that overlap a rival only once are removed. Diversity: mean cosine similarity of pairs of rows of a region (pdd) and share of pairs that are functionally identical (tc). Marked: from the thresholds of quality.highlight.

Pairs of regions

Each pair of regions of different classes that is near (separation under ), shares conflicting rows, or is a region's nearest rival, measured on at most rows per region: kDN and ENN (neighbours of the other region), N1 (rows on the spanning tree's border), F3 (rows one feature tells apart, and which), L2 (error of a linear boundary: high with a low overlap, the boundary is curved). "without shortcuts": the same without the shortcut columns.
