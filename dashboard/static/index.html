Classes

Rows equal on every column, the class included, are kept once; n_duplicates counts their copies (1 when unique) and is the row's weight in the regions, not a feature. Then the classes under min_class_count distinct rows are dropped. Rows with a missing or infinite value are kept: stage prepare puts them on the mean.

Numeric columns

Each number becomes sign(x)·log1p(|x|), then is standardized with the mean and standard deviation below (of the logged finite values); a missing or infinite one lands on the mean, 0, and the n_missing column counts, unscaled, how many numbers of the row did (present even when none). It is a diagnostic: no distance sees it.

Categorical slots

The top_n most frequent values of a column (a missing one counts as a value) get their own one-hot slot; the others share the <other> slot. The active slot weighs sqrt(cat_cost / g), with g the chance two rows differ on the column: on average a column adds cat_cost times what a number adds, and a mismatch on a concentrated column costs more (2·weight² in squared distance).

Tree of a class

Each class is split on its own by a Ward tree of its distinct rows, each weighted by its copies in the CSV. Every branch is cut where the loss is least: the hardness spread a region hides (kDN variance) plus the sampling noise of its mean hardness. A branch under min_cluster_floor rows of the sample is pruned; its rows, and the rows outside the sample of the tree, take the region of their nearest labelled rows by vote. The tree shows the splits the cut keeps; each node counts the distinct rows of its regions (hover for the rows of the CSV). Click a node to map its rows.

Map

Overlaps between classes

The regions of every class, weighted by their CSV rows, grow one more Ward tree with the class ignored: it continues the trees of the classes from their regions, so classes whose regions lie close meet low in it. Each branch takes the colour of its class while it holds one; a dot marks where classes meet and its height is the cost of that merge (log scale). The ratio of a merge is the distance between its two sides' centroids over the sum of their radii (the root mean square distance of their rows from their centroid): under 1 the two spreads overlap. Click a node to map its rows and find its branch in each class tree.

Where two classes meet

One row per node where a class from one side meets a class from the other and neither side holds both; branch is the node of the class tree above all its regions there. Click a row to select it.

Granularity per class

The cut of least loss per class, and its loss against its regions when a penalty per region coarsens it (the penalty is above 0 only when the class had to be capped at max_regions).
