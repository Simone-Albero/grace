Numeric columns

Each number becomes sign(x)·log1p(|x|), then is standardized with the mean and standard deviation below (of the logged finite values); a missing or infinite one lands on the mean, 0, and the n_missing column counts, unscaled, how many numbers of the row did (present even when none). It is a diagnostic: no distance sees it.

Categorical slots

The top_n most frequent values of a column (a missing one counts as a value) get their own one-hot slot; the others share the <other> slot. The active slot weighs sqrt(cat_cost / g), with g the chance two rows differ on the column: on average a column adds cat_cost times what a number adds, and a mismatch on a concentrated column costs more (2·weight² in squared distance).

Tree of a class

Each class is split on its own, by geometry alone. A Ward tree of half of its distinct rows (each weighted by its copies in the CSV) proposes the splits; the other half judges them, so a split is never judged on the rows that made it. The separation of the two sides of a split is the distance between their centroids in standard deviations of the other half's rows along the line between them, on the numbers only and each distinct row counted once, less two standard errors (Ashman's D over √2: a measure of how far apart, not a test). The tree is cut from the leaves up wherever a split reaches split_separation, then the pieces not that far apart are sewn back, the closest pair first. A branch under min_cluster_floor rows of the judging half is pruned; its rows, and the rows outside the two halves, take the region of their nearest labelled rows by vote. The tree drawn here is a Ward tree of the regions' centroids: it shows how the regions group, its leaves are the regions; each node counts their distinct rows (hover for the rows of the CSV). Overlaps between classes

Judged on the regions alone. The separation of two regions is the distance between their centroids in standard deviations of their rows along the line between them (√ of Fisher's ratio on that line): two regions of different classes overlap when it is under overlap_separation. Single linkage on these pairs, the closest first, groups the regions into zones; a region close to no other class is in none, and classes that never come that close never meet. Each zone is drawn as its tree: leaves in the colour of their class, each merge at the separation of the pair that made it. Click a node to map its rows and find its branch in each class tree.

Pairs of regions that overlap

Every pair of regions of different classes under overlap_separation, not only those that made a merge; sd a and sd b are the standard deviations of each region's rows along the line between the centroids. Click a row to select the lowest node above both regions.

Granularity per class

Pieces cut are the regions before sewing; sewn at is the separation the regions keep, split_separation unless the class had to be capped at max_regions (then marked). Stability: the regions are made again on other halves and each is matched to its most similar new region (Jaccard, Hennig 2007), stable from 0.75, doubtful from 0.6, unreliable under it; a class's is the mean of its regions', by rows. Stable is not proof: an unstable region is suspect, a stable one is not sure. The curve is how many regions the same tree gives at other thresholds.

Regions

Select a dataset.

How to read
rows, CSV rows
Rows are the distinct rows stage clean kept; CSV rows count their copies too. Every measure is computed on distinct rows; shares of the dataset are in CSV rows.
status
A region is solid when it has none of the problems below, flagged otherwise; its problems are listed in the order missing, duplicates, label_conflict, class_overlap, shortcut.
missing
At least one cell of the region is empty, not a number where a number is due (unparsable), NaN or infinite. A single one is enough.
duplicates
Copies: the share of the region's CSV rows that repeat another row exactly, label included. Flagged from .
label_conflict
Rows identical on every feature, as read from the CSV, but labelled with different classes: no classifier can tell them apart. Flagged with a single such row.
neighbours
The regions of other classes a region is measured against: those whose centroid is closer than sd (near), those sharing identical rows (label_conflict), and the closest region of another class (nearest). Each pair is measured on at most random rows per region.
nn_error
The share of a region's rows whose nearest row (itself excluded) belongs to the other region: 0 when the two are apart, about 0.5 when they are mixed. Measured on each side; the pair's is the mean of the two, so a large region does not hide a small one. bayes_error_low is the least error any classifier could reach, from nn_error (Cover & Hart; an approximation on few rows).
shortcut_columns
Up to columns removed one at a time, each the one whose removal raises nn_error the most (chosen on half of the rows, measured on the other half): the columns the separation depends on. nn_error without is the error once they are removed.
single_column_acc
The balanced accuracy of the best rule on one column alone: a threshold on a number, a value-to-class map on a categorical (OneR, Holte 1993; Ho & Basu's F3, made robust to outliers). A column separates alone from .
verdict of a pair
With τ = : label_conflict when nn_error ≥ τ and the two share identical rows; class_overlap when nn_error ≥ τ and no single column separates them; shortcut when removing the shortcut columns raises nn_error by τ or more, up to τ at least: the separation hangs on those columns; robust otherwise: separated, also without them. A region takes the class_overlap and shortcut problems from any of its pairs.
pdd, tc (classes only)
pdd: the mean cosine similarity of random pairs of rows of the class (low_diversity from ); tc: the share of those pairs that are functionally identical, cosine over 0.95 (collapse from ). After Flood et al., EuroS&P 2024. Judged on classes only: a region is alike by construction.
