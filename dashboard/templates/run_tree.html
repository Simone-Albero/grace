Each predicted class has a tree of k-means splits (2 to tree.max_k parts) in the LDA space, grown on one half of val. A leaf is a region: its estimate q is the failure rate of the other half of val routed to it, shrunk toward its rate on the grown half. Click a marker, or a leaf id, for its page.

Tree of a class

A square is a leaf, a circle a node that splits; size follows the grown rows. Color shows the measure chosen below on one scale for all of them, the failure rate of val: the middle is the val rate at the top of the tree, blue is below it and red above, and the ends are the 2nd and 98th percentiles of the leaves. The predicted rate is the leaf's q, and a node that splits takes the mean of its leaves' weighted by their val rows; purity is the share of grown rows the classifier got right, red where it is low. Hover a node for its rows and rates in val (both halves) and test, click a leaf for its page.

The map under the tree draws a sample of the grown rows of each leaf, at most a thousand of them thinned to the same count per leaf, in the plane of the first two principal components of the class's grown rows, in the LDA space z or, when picked, in z with s_d, the disagreement of the neighbours standardized and weighted as the tree grew on it, each point in the color of its leaf; the view leaves out the farthest 1% on each side, and the autoscale button of the chart shows all. The tree also cuts on s_d, an axis the plane of z alone does not show. Click a node that splits to cut the tree down to its subtree, with the map refitted to the rows below it; Reset, or the class in the path above the tree, brings back the whole.
