What the scores mean

MSE ratio
The mean squared error of the probability against each row's failure (0 or 1), over that of the constant val failure rate. Lower is better and below 1 beats the constant. It never reaches 0, since a single failure is partly chance.
Binned MSE ratio
The rows are grouped into 10 bins of equal size by the predictor's probability. It compares each bin's mean probability with its actual failure rate, over the same gap for the val rate. A row's own chance drops out, but the noise of each bin's rate stays, so compare predictors within a run, not across test sizes. Lower is better.
AUROC
The chance that a random failed row gets a higher probability than a random correct one. 0.5 is a random order and 1 puts every failure first. It does not depend on the failure rate. Higher is better.
AP / p
The average precision, i.e. the share of failures among the rows ranked above each failure, averaged over the failures and divided by the failure rate p. It rewards a good top of the ranking. 1 is a random order and the best is 1 / p, so compare predictors within a run. Higher is better.
